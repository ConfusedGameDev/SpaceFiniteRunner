SpaceFiniteRunner Systems

Two games share one Unity 6 project: the Finite Runner, a hover-ship police chase on a finite track, and the City chase, a car chase through a baked cyberpunk city. A mission is one city level followed by one runner escape. This document keeps the two games apart, shows where every value is tuned, and marks what the single-source refactor has changed so far and what it will change next.

Rules that hold everywhere

  • Gameplay never writes a settings asset; the debug menu does. Gameplay takes a runtime clone where it changes values. A debug row writes the asset, then mirrors the value onto the live clone. What the inspector shows is what runs. Changed · Step 2
  • Tunables live in ScriptableObjects, drawn with Odin sliders and inlined into the component that uses them.
  • Scene-lifetime systems are hand-placed under ===SYSTEMS===. Code only finds or parks them.
  • Domain reload is off. Static state and event subscriptions survive between play sessions.
  • Speeds are stored in m/s. The UI converts with × 3.6.
Part A · Finite Runner

The hover-ship escape run

Scene 05.Scenes/FiniteRunner_TestCode 01.Scripts/Runner + 01.Scripts/ShipData 04.Data/FiniteRunner + 04.Data/Resources

How a run plays

  • Win: every mandatory objective of the run's RunnerLevelDefinition is met (today one Reach Speed objective, the HUD's "Light Speed", which latches once reached) and the ship leaves the track off one of the three end ramps. The ship flies on, MISSION ACCOMPLISHED slams in, the glitch ramps to max, then the Mission Complete panel opens.
  • Lose: the countdown hits 0, the patrol catches you, you reach the end with an objective open or through a gap between the ramps, or the hull reaches 0 and the ship explodes. Every loss shows MISSION FAILED, then the retry panel. Standing still never loses.
  • Hull and lives: wall slams, wall scrapes, lasers and falls cost hull, and every hit gives a short invulnerable blink. Repair orbs heal 15 %. Every failed run costs a life. Losing the last one is GAME OVER: the wallet goes back to the mission start, the city clear is dropped, and you return to the Store.
  • Speed: one launch, then the throttle holds you up to cruise speed. Only boost orbs (green ×1, blue ×2.5, purple ×10) and ramp lips push past cruise, where a passive bleed pulls you back down. A laser hit costs 10 % of speed. There is no cap.
  • Timed boost: press Boost (A / Space) as you cross an orb: ×1.5 dead on, down to ×1.1 at the edge of a ±0.15 s window.
  • The patrol duel: the cruiser holds a standoff, then commits to an attack run on your flank, takes your controls and opens a tug of war. Win it and one press of the right dash shoulder destroys the cruiser. Brake in its standoff and it overshoots. A laser destroys it. A blue or purple orb leaves you armed, which skips straight to the kill.
  • Falls: flat sweeps and open straights have no outer wall. Over the edge the ship falls, then returns further down the track at 85 % of its speed, blinking for 3 s with the patrol held. The countdown keeps running.
  • Features: jump ramps, vertical loops, full tubes and laser gates. The track ends in a 1,200 m walled run-up and three ramps over a void.

How it is built

The ship is split in two. The Ship assembly is a standalone hover ship that flies any collider surface and knows nothing about the track. The Runner assembly puts the runner's rules on top.

HoverShip + HoverBody Ship asm

A kinematic, cast-based surface follower. Each physics tick is split into ≤ 4 m substeps, re-seated with probe rays, with walls handled by hull sweeps. Never a dynamic rigidbody.

A run from boot to retry

  1. TrackGenerator.Awake clones the shape and spawner assets and builds the first stretch.
  2. GameManager.Awake subscribes to events, adds the ship components, builds the run definition (clone → Store upgrades), deals the lives, inits the patrol (clone), attaches the camera and applies FX and music.
  3. ShipMotor.Start launches from the track's start pose.
  4. Each tick: the ship steps, the motor mirrors it and applies runner rules, the patrol steps its body and the duel, GameManager ticks the countdown and objectives, the generator streams and culls.
  5. Win: the ship reaches the end on a ramp with objectives met → banner → glitch → MissionCompleteScreen pays and offers NEXT MISSION / RETRY / EXIT.
  6. Lose: a life is taken → banner → GameOverScreen. RETRY restarts in place: the track regenerates, ship and patrol relaunch, hull refills. On the last life the mission is forfeited and you go to the Store.

Part B · City chase

The car chase through a baked city

Scene 05.Scenes/CarTestCode 01.Scripts/PoliceEscapeData 04.Data/InfiniteCity + 04.Data/Resources
CarTest is the level. CityTest is not. CarTest holds the baked city and every system, and it is the scene World 01 loads. CityTest is an old harness with no baked city: its CityManager only warns "no CityRoot". The city play-checks in this refactor run in CarTest.

How a level plays

  • A mission brief opens and you accept optional challenges. Then the LevelDefinition objectives play in order, each briefed by an RPG line and shown on the ObjectiveHud.
  • Objective types: Reach Speed, Escape Police, Go To Target, Survive Time, Chase Car, Destroy Cars, Collect Objects, Jump.
  • The car spawns rolling at a PlayerSpawnPoint.
  • Damage is the full-screen glitch. Hard impacts pulse it. Each police hit adds 0.34 for good, so three hits end the run. Barrels and water splashes also damage you. The Store's Resistance upgrade divides every hit.
  • Losing: full corruption, a missed deadline, or a flipped car. The glitch holds, then the retry panel opens. RETRY restarts in place without a scene reload.
  • Completing: the glitch goes to max and the runner loads additively. The city banks no money itself; the runner pays the whole mission.

City generation: baked, not streamed

The city is generated offline into 03.Prefabs/PoliceEscape/City.prefab. Play mode generates nothing.

  • CityDefinition holds the grid, seed and one entry per block (override settings, connector-only, water, district). CityGenerationSettings holds only city-wide knobs and BlockSettings only interior knobs. That split keeps block borders connected.
  • CityLayout is the pure model. Arterials come from a periodic field, so neighbouring blocks agree on their borders, including across the wrap seam.
  • CityBaker runs RoadNetworkGenerator → RoadFeaturePlacer (overpasses, forks, roundabouts, curved avenues) → CityBlockBuilder, then buildings, props, parks and shorelines. Edit and rebake from Tools → Police Escape → City Designer.
  • At runtime CityRoot rebuilds the RoadGraph from the baked layouts and answers spatial queries. CityStreamer switches whole blocks on and off by distance (enter 500 m / exit 650 m). CityWrap teleports the player across the edge. CityBounds limits where NPCs spawn.

Part D · What changed
